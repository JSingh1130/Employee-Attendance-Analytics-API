1. **Indexes.** Employees use a unique `emp_code` index, plus department/code and join-date indexes for listing, headcount, and trend queries. Attendance uses a unique `(emp_code, date)` index for the natural key, date/code for listing, employee/date for monthly lookups, employee/punch-in for punch-out selection, status/date for filtered lists, and date/late-minutes for leaderboard aggregation. I rejected a separate attendance `emp_code` index because the unique compound index already has that prefix.
2. **Punch-in race.** Both requests may read the employee, but MongoDB's unique `(emp_code, date)` index allows only one insert. The winner returns 201; the duplicate-key loser returns 409. There is no check-then-insert correctness dependency.
3. **Ties.** `$rank` assigns equal late totals the same rank. The limit filter runs after ranking, so all employees tied at a rank within the cutoff are returned; rows then sort by late total and `emp_code`.
4. **Headcount.** Department summary starts from employees joined by period end and looks up logs, preserving employees with no records. Later joiners are filtered out before aggregation.
5. **100x data.** I would move repeated monthly analytics to incrementally maintained summaries, retaining raw attendance as the source of truth and checking the summaries against aggregation results.
